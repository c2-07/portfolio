# Entropy

*Everything tends towards disorder unless energy is put into it.*

It's been almost a year and a half, and a lot has changed during this time. I've transitioned from school to college, and honestly, I feel like I wasted most of my first year without doing much to improve my programming skills or knowledge. I kept thinking I had plenty of time and that I could always start tomorrow. Well, tomorrow never really came.

Looking back, I think I spent a lot of time convincing myself that I was just lazy and not hardworking enough. It was an easy excuse, really. Instead of figuring out why I wasn't doing the things I wanted to do, I just labelled myself as lazy and moved on. I kept procrastinating, convincing myself that there was always enough time to catch up.

I even started comparing myself to my peers, thinking, *"I know more than them, so I'm doing fine. I'll always be ahead of them."* Somewhere along the way, that false sense of superiority became another excuse to stop pushing myself. I was so busy convincing myself that I was ahead that I stopped making any actual progress.

During the semester break in December, I kind of disconnected from everything and reconnected with some friends on Discord. I started learning from them, asking questions, and figuring out what I actually wanted to do. I spent almost the entire month exploring web development.

I'd always avoided web development because I never really enjoyed frontend stuff, but one of my friends inspired me to give it a shot. What really caught my attention was that he didn't even know how to use the framework he was working with. He just wanted to build something, so he started experimenting with it. He wasn't constantly questioning whether he was good enough or whether learning it was even worth his time. He just did it.

And it wasn't just him. Everyone in the group kept experimenting with new technologies, building random stuff, and sharing what they learned with everyone else. Whenever someone built something cool or figured something out, they'd share it on the server. Others would get inspired and try it themselves. There was this constant cycle of learning, building, sharing, and motivating each other. Nobody really needed a roadmap or someone to tell them what to learn next.

I think that's when something clicked for me. Maybe the problem wasn't entirely me. Maybe the environment I was in mattered more than I realised.

When I returned to college after the break, I started noticing how different the environment felt. Most people around me seemed pretty unmotivated and directionless. Everyone was just following what their bhaiyas or seniors had told them to do. Grind DSA, learn the MERN stack, memorise system design, and somehow that'll get you an internship.

I tried keeping my distance from that mindset, but it's honestly difficult when you're surrounded by it every day. People constantly ask for roadmaps but barely follow through with anything. There's always a new technology to learn, a new roadmap to follow, or someone else's knowledge to question. Eventually, you find yourself getting dragged into the same cycle you were trying to get away from.

It's especially difficult when you don't really have anyone to look up to. When you feel like you've already explored more than most people in your year, finding someone to learn from becomes surprisingly hard. Outside the internet, there's barely anyone around to have genuinely interesting conversations about tech with. Even in the tech clubs, most conversations seem to revolve around the latest AI website, the whole "10x engineer" hype, and whatever technology is trending this week. There are probably some genuinely good people around, but most seem to be vibe coders. Nothing wrong with that, IMO, but there's barely anything to learn from them except how to copy-paste prompts. And then there are the larpers who always have a lot to say but very little to show for it.

Sometimes I just want to sit down with someone, talk about how something actually works, exchange ideas, argue about different approaches, and build something together. Not everything has to be about internships, placements, or adding another technology to your resume.

By the time I reached my third semester, I was honestly fed up with all of this. I tried to keep working on my own projects and learning whatever interested me, but staying motivated in college was harder than I expected. I failed at it plenty of times. There were days when I did absolutely nothing, and days when I managed to get things done. But I guess those little wins count too.

Now I'm back home, working on my little `tiny-redis` implementation, and damn, it feels good to just sit down and write code by hand again. No roadmaps, no chasing trends, no constantly worrying about what I should learn next. Just me, a problem, and a bunch of code I'm trying to figure out.

Maybe that's what I've been missing all this time: the simple joy of figuring things out on my own.

I keep coming back to the idea of entropy. Left on its own, everything tends towards disorder. Maybe that's a decent way to describe what happened during my first year. I had time, opportunities, and curiosity, but without putting any consistent energy into them, everything slowly became scattered.

And I don't think the answer is to suddenly become disciplined, productive, or obsessed with getting ahead of everyone else. At least, that's not what I want anymore.

I want to explore things because they interest me. Build things because I want to understand how they work. Learn without constantly worrying about whether it's useful or whether someone else is already better at it.

Maybe I can't control all the chaos around me, but I can decide where to put my energy.

**Perhaps this journal is my little attempt at doing exactly that: making some order out of the entropy.**
